Blood Donation FAQs

Find answers to your common questions

1. Who can donate blood?

Generally, healthy individuals between 18 and 65 years with suitable weight and medical conditions can donate blood.

2. How often can I donate blood?

A person can usually donate blood every 3 to 4 months depending on health conditions and guidelines.

3. Is blood donation safe?

Yes. Blood donation is a safe process performed using sterile equipment by trained medical professionals.

4. Does donating blood make me weak?

No. Your body quickly replaces the donated blood. Drinking fluids and resting helps recovery.

5. How much blood is donated?

A standard blood donation collects around 350-450 ml of blood depending on donor eligibility.

6. Can I donate blood during an emergency?

Yes. Registered donors can be contacted quickly when their blood group matches an emergency request.

7. Which blood group is universal donor?

O negative blood is considered the universal red blood cell donor type.

8. Which blood group is universal receiver?

AB positive can receive red blood cells from all blood groups.
